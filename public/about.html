One garage. One pair of hands.

Sato Garage is Valor Hirsch — pressing, shaping and finishing fingerboard decks one at a time, at home in the USA.

The story

It started on a garage bench

One press, a stack of veneer and a lot of decks that did not work before the ones that did.

Fingerboarding is a small world, and the good stuff has always come out of garages rather than factories. Learning to press a deck properly — the right glue-up, the right clamp pressure, a concave that actually feels like something under your fingers — takes a pile of ruined wood first. That pile is the reason the current ones come out right.

Everything still gets made the same way: a few decks at a time, cut and sanded by hand, checked before they go anywhere. No batch of a thousand, no shortcuts to keep a shelf stocked.

How I work

The things I will not cut

Press it properly

A rushed glue-up delaminates eventually. If a deck comes out of the press wrong, it does not get sold — it gets pressed again.

Say the hard thing

If an idea will not work in wood, or the budget will not stretch to it, you hear that up front rather than after it is made.

Your deck, your call

I will give you an opinion on width and concave every time you ask. The final call on all of it is still yours.

Nothing hidden

You get the price before I start, and photos as it comes together — including the stages that look rough.

Take on less

A short queue means your deck actually gets made, instead of sitting on a list behind two hundred others.

Stand behind it

If a deck I made comes apart on its own, tell me. That conversation does not need to be a fight.

What gets made here

It changes with whatever people ask for, but most orders land somewhere in here.

  • Custom widths from 32 mm up to 35 mm
  • Mellow through to deep concave, your call
  • 5-, 6- and 7-ply maple builds
  • Exotic veneer tops and bottoms
  • Stains, solid colours and custom graphics
  • One-off shapes and decks made as gifts
